How elo101 reviews a chess game

A chess engine scores a position with one number, whoever is playing. elo101 turns that number into what really matters: how often players like you score from here.

The idea: the same position isn't the same for everyone

In one famous position the engine says White is clearly winning (+5.2), but the win needs a rook sacrifice and then a queen sacrifice. elo101's model gives White a 93% chance for two 2400-rated players, 84% for two 1600s and 69% for two 800s, in a 10-minute game. A plain engine score treats all three the same.

Expected points

elo101 predicts each player's expected points from a position: a win is 1, a draw ½, a loss 0. The model (gradient-boosted trees, LightGBM) learned this from millions of real Lichess games, using the Stockfish evaluation, both ratings, the time control, both clocks and the stage of the game. On positions from games it never saw, it predicts results 11.5% better than the formula Lichess uses, and its percentages are calibrated: when it says 70%, players score about 70%.

From expected points to move labels

  1. Stockfish scores the position after your move, and after the best move.
  2. The model turns both scores into expected points for players of your rating.
  3. The difference is what your move cost. Small costs are Excellent or Good; large ones are Mistakes or Blunders. Special checks find Brilliant sacrifices, Great only-moves and Misses. All labels →

Moves are judged by a model that looks only at the board, not the clock, so hanging a queen in a bullet game is still a blunder.

Accuracy and game rating

Accuracy uses Lichess's published formula, which weighs moves in sharp positions more and isn't hidden by one big blunder among good moves. Game rating estimates what rating each player played like in this game, from their accuracy and the players' average rating. The more accurate player never gets the lower game rating.

Runs in your browser

Stockfish 18 runs as WebAssembly on every CPU core, and the trained model runs as plain JavaScript. Your game is never sent to a server for analysis, which is why elo101 can be free with no limit.

Tested in the open

Every change is checked against a fixed benchmark of 450 real games, 300 puzzles and 64 hand-checked positions. The code, the model and the full results are on GitHub.

Questions

What are expected points in chess?

Expected points is the score a player can expect from a position: 1 for a win, ½ for a draw and 0 for a loss, averaged over how games like it actually end. 0.70 means about 70%.

How is elo101's accuracy calculated?

With Lichess's published accuracy formula, applied to elo101's own Stockfish evaluations in your browser.

Is elo101 open source?

Yes. The code, model training pipeline and benchmark results are on GitHub under the MIT license.
